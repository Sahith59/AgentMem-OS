# Interpretation of the completed attempts

The fixed Sarvam-105B profile improved from 90/120 to 105/120 source-correct date/clarification answers. Twenty pairs improved and five regressed. Fourteen improvements recovered a previously unusable reporter output; six improved a previously usable but wrong answer. This supports a narrower conclusion about reporting reliability, not a new memory architecture. Usable finals increased from 100 to 116. Nine candidate answers have the correct null date but the wrong clarification flag; date-only 114/120 is supplementary and must not replace the frozen primary 105/120 headline.

Conversations improved only from 95/120 to 96/120, with four gains and three losses. Non-null wrong dates increased from 18 to 22, directly violating the frozen no-increase criterion. Strict task success decreased from 85 to 84 because truthful answers on episodes with unnecessary action attempts cannot count as strict success. No pooled score may hide these regressions.

Two separate conditions fail the acceptance gate: the native cutoff left two reporter slots unreached, and Conversations made more non-null wrong date assertions. The candidate is not promoted. Completing all 240 native attempts is not the same as completing all 480 reporter calls: 478 were issued, with 2 unreached slots retained as failures.

There are concrete remaining source-present failures. Candidate Conversations misses all 12 preferred-date questions that involve two booking references, despite the unchanged target preference being supplied explicitly. It also misses 7 of 12 unresolved-condition questions and 2 of 12 resolved-condition questions. Across models, some null/clarification misses are contract disagreements, not invented dates; these are counted and described separately. The review packet records the exact source, not just a category label. These outcomes implicate question/source interpretation in this integration, but do not by themselves identify model reasoning, prior actor prose or input layout as the cause.

The actor-stage gap is directly traceable: one successful HTTP response had `finish_reason=length`, no content and no tool calls, so the actor validator returned `invalid_action_response` before the reporting branch. No backend action occurred. A separate no-tools reporting handoff is a reasonable bounded recovery hypothesis; no live gain follows until it is measured. The original episode remains failed and unchanged.

Backend enforcement prevented forbidden state changes in this test. Of 48 required new writes, 47 completed; Conversations missed one legitimate request near the synthetic approval boundary. Conversations made 11 unnecessary attempts, which the host rejected; the base model made none. Frozen server time and synthetic approval identities are experiment controls, not production authentication or real-time expiry validation.

All relevant source information was supplied in these cases. We have not demonstrated a missing-persistence problem or a unique multilingual memory advantage. This is a useful integration finding with a stronger measured base-model result and clear remaining failures. It is not proof of product novelty, coverage of all Sarvam languages, a production-ready system, or improvement on the separate English LongMemEval baseline.

# Hindi semantic review of the three reporter views

The [assistant review](hindi-review.json) covers all 240 planned Hindi finals: 40 source scenarios × two fixed Sarvam profiles × three arms. I read the Hindi questions and source conditions, checked the saved execution receipts and current scoped booking for each model history, and compared every raw output with the offline receipt replay. There were **zero assistant/scorer disagreements** and no unresolved source-label ambiguity on this slice. Six capped/incomplete finals remain failures. This is an assistant language review, not an independent native-speaker assessment.

| Model, 40 histories each | A chronological | B grouped | C grouped, actor prose withheld |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sarvam 105B: correct date + clarification | 33 | 33 | 36 |
| Sarvam 105B: correct raw action status | 29 | 31 | 32 |
| Conversations: correct date + clarification | 31 | 35 | 34 |
| Conversations: correct raw action status | 36 | 32 | 32 |

Paired factual movements on **all 40 histories per model** were: base A→B 3 gains/3 losses, B→C 5/2, A→C 5/2; Conversations A→B 4/0, B→C 4/5, A→C 6/3. The predeclared *treated* C−B contrast excludes byte-identical no-op requests: base 5 gains/1 loss across 34 histories; Conversations 4 gains/5 losses across 29. One additional base B→C loss was a capped C final for `26-hi-m0` even though B and C request bytes were identical there; it is retained in all-history counts and cannot be credited to prose removal. Every one of the 24 Hindi explicit replacement-request finals (01–04, both models, all arms) answered the latest requested date; this does not reproduce the English C abstentions reported for that family.

The gains and regressions have different user-facing meanings. On Conversations separate-booking cases 17–20, C recovers all four target preferences that B misstated as the current booked date. Yet on known morning-availability cases 09–12, C falsely asks for clarification in all four Conversations histories, versus one for B and two for A. On unknown-availability cases 13 and 15, C chooses an unsupported conditional date; B makes no such definite guess in Conversations. Conversations raw status accuracy also falls from 36 under A to 32 under both B and C, although the application-owned projection remains 40/40. Base C improves several missing-date and source-scoping answers, but its invalid finals include treated `37-hi-m0` and the no-op `26-hi-m0`. The Hindi treated C−B direction is therefore **negative for Conversations**. On this slice C also has more false abstentions and lower raw-status accuracy than the ordinary chronological control. These are local observations; the frozen [protocol](../PROTOCOL.md) and full EN/HI/TE gate decide the formal campaign result.

The Hindi evidence supports a narrow source-interpretation problem and shows that rearranging evidence or omitting actor prose can change answers under fixed models. It does **not** support blanket prose omission as a reliable product feature, establish a missing persistence layer, or distinguish a novel memory system. The relevant facts were already present; the [prior-art update](../PRIOR_ART_UPDATE.md) also documents existing temporal, provenance and typed-fact capabilities. Any next product test should first beat a strong ordinary control for source scoping, conditional-premise handling, and application-owned action status on fresh, independently reviewed workflows at declared cost and latency. No new experiment is authorized by this slice note.
